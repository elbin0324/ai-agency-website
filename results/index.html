04How we estimate

The rules behind every number.

Every figure on this site follows them. If a number can’t meet them, it doesn’t appear.

Our rules

  1. 01

    Conservative by default.

    When an assumption could go either way, we take the one that makes the savings smaller.

  2. 02

    Specific tasks, not whole jobs.

    Agents take on defined workflows, like routine replies or data entry, and people keep the judgment calls. Time spent reviewing their work comes off first.

  3. 03

    Fully loaded cost.

    Staff time is valued at the median U.S. wage for the role plus payroll taxes and benefits, from BLS data. Nothing is added for office space or overhead.

  4. 04

    Hours aren’t dollars until they are.

    Time back is capacity. The calculator and the worked examples value it at what it costs you; the industry scenarios count half, because not every freed hour becomes paid work.

  5. 05

    Gross profit, not revenue.

    Extra jobs, patients or orders count only at their margin, never at their full price.

  6. 06

    Before our fees.

    Estimates don’t subtract our fees, because fees depend on scope. Your proposal shows net savings and payback.

How we model

The savings calculator

Counts two kinds of work: repetitive desk tasks handed to agents, and routine customer inquiries an agent resolves on its own. It values only the time returned, at the loaded hourly cost you enter.

Desk hours = team × hours a week × share × 80% × 50 ÷ 12Inquiry hours = inquiries × 5 min × 20% ÷ 60Value a year = hours back × 12 × hourly costCapacity = hours back × 12 ÷ 1,880

Worked examples, one per service

A fictional business for each service. Task time savings use base rates from published studies, already net of review. Work an agent completes on its own, like a customer conversation, has 20% set aside for review and handoffs.

Monthly value = hours back × loaded cost + gross profit or savingsAnnual value = monthly value × 12, at 4 working weeks a month

Industry scenarios

Six fictional businesses from 30 to 250 staff, with the agents each would use. Freed hours count at half their cost, and new business only at its gross margin.

Annual value = hours back × loaded cost × 50% + gross profit + cost savings

Every value is the base case from the evidence, or the conservative one when a claim leads a page.
