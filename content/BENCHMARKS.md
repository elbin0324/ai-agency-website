# Benchmarks: the evidence behind every number

Generated from verified research (every figure below was opened at its source by an independent checker; unverifiable figures were dropped). Researched 2026-10-12/13.

## How to use this file

- Public numbers use the **base** value, or **conservative** when a claim is a headline. Never the optimistic value in a headline.
- Savings are task-level (specific workflows such as drafting routine replies), never whole-job replacement.
- Every outcome on the site is labeled "Illustrative example" or "Modeled estimate", and the results page methodology cites the source ids below.
- Population studies find small average whole-job gains (about 3% of hours in Denmark; 1.4-2.0 h/week of email time saved at large firms), while controlled task studies find large gains on specific tasks. Frame savings as specific workflows handed to agents, not as general productivity multipliers.
- Consulting comparison: compare price and time **for the same scope of work** (a diagnostic plus a prioritized roadmap). Never claim "similar results". GSA figures are "GSA schedule hourly ceiling rates (federal contracts)": federal not-to-exceed ceilings, not what firms charge small businesses.

## Do not use

Commonly cited figures that could not be traced to a primary source with a method, or that mislead in context.

### Labor costs

- **'AI Consulting delivers the same (or similar) result as a consulting firm' or 'same quality at a fraction of the cost', especially naming a firm such as McKinsey or BCG** Nothing in this evidence measures consulting outcomes, so a 'similar result' claim has no basis. The FTC's advertising-substantiation policy (Nov 23, 1984) requires 'a reasonable basis for advertising claims before they are disseminated'.
- **Named-firm GSA ceiling rates (McKinsey, BCG, KPMG, EY) presented as what those firms charge commercial or small-business clients, as full-team or partner rates, or as typical of 'consulting firms' in…** They are federal not-to-exceed ceilings for the listed categories only. McKinsey's schedule has 10 analyst, expert, design and support categories and no partner or engagement-manager rows.
- **GSA CALC+ MAS rates described in public copy as 'fully burdened' or as 'what consultants charge'** GSA's CALC+ guide calls them 'not-to-exceed "ceiling" prices' that 'should not be viewed as exact estimates' for a specific order. The 'fully burdened' wording was found only on third-party sites and on GSA's OASIS+ page (a different vehicle).
- **'Federal GSA ceilings are typically at or below commercial rates' stated as fact** GSAM 538.270-1 says only that GSA 'will seek to obtain the offeror's best price', and it allows 'legitimate reasons why the best price is not achieved'. That is policy intent, not a measured comparison.
- **Consultant and fractional billing rates 'run 2 to 3 times' employee wages (research note on the accountants/analysts comparator)** No source. Computed cross-source ratios range from about 2.2x (Go Fractional CFO average vs BLS financial-manager median) to about 3.4x (GSA small-firm management-consultant median vs BLS management-analyst median), and the pairs are different populations.
- **Financial and investment analyst wages cited to OEWS Table 1** Table 1 has no such row. It shows the broader 'Financial analysts and advisors' (median $49.08, mean $61.39). Cite the 13-2051 national estimate (median $49.40).
- **Go Fractional rates quoted without a date, as fixed facts, or as US-only (including the researcher's $178/hr, $125-$210 band, $180 ask and $7,000-$11,700/month)** It is a rolling 90-day live benchmark that changed between the research read and the 2026-10-13 update (average now $177, band $125-$200, asks $179, monthly $7,000-$11,200). Go Fractional serves the US, UK and Europe and does not state the sample's geography.
- **GSA medians quoted to the cent from the API's 'median_price' or percentile aggregations** Those are approximate percentiles that drift between queries (small-firm business consultant: $143.07 at research time, $143.93 now, $143.55 exact). Quote exact recomputed medians rounded to whole dollars.
- **SDR fully loaded cost of about $98K presented as typical for small businesses** That figure is the Bridge Group median OTE (83% SaaS sample, $47M median revenue, full quota attainment assumed; only 60% of SDRs hit quota) times the ECEC load. Use the BLS-based $86K as the base and present $98K only as the SaaS-survey upper case.
- **Small-business loaded costs built with the all-private ECEC ratio (1.43 per hour, 1.29 per year)** Large establishments pull the all-private average up. The measured small-establishment range is 1.35-1.375 per hour worked and 1.245-1.26 on annual pay.
- **CPA-firm partner billing rates of '$368 to $446 per hour' (attributed to the 2025 Rosenberg MAP Survey)** Seen only in a search-engine summary. The publisher's public page (cpatrendlines.com/shop/map-survey) does not show it, and the report is paywalled, so it cannot be traced.
- **AICPA PCPS MAP Survey hourly billing rates by position** The 2025 public release gives sample size (1,073 completed), fee growth and pay, but no billing-rate figures. Rates are member-only, so nothing citable.
- **Go Fractional industry-specific CFO rates ($195/hr financial services, $199/hr professional services, $203/hr construction)** Seen only in search snippets. The pages returned 403 to WebFetch and were not checked. Industry slices of an already small vendor sample are not defensible.
- **QuickBooks Live Full-Service Bookkeeping tier prices ($300/$500/$700 or $300/$400/$500+ per month)** Third-party reviews disagree, and Intuit's own pages did not show prices in fetchable content (direct access was blocked). Unverified.
- **Consulting Success 'Consulting Fees Study' as a source for consultant hourly rates** The page publishes no hourly, daily or project rates and no survey year. The sample is global and self-reported, so it supports no rate claim.
- **Rule of thumb that an employee costs 1.25-1.4x salary (often credited to MIT/Hadzima) or 'add 30% for benefits'** No disclosed method, and it mixes bases. ECEC measures it directly: benefits are 30% of total compensation.
- **Annual cost = hourly wage x ECEC multiplier x 2,080, or hourly cost = loaded annual cost / 1,776 productive hours** Both count paid leave twice, which inflates costs by about 10-13%. Value saved hours at hourly wage x ECEC multiplier. For annual role costs use annual pay x 1.24 (small clients) or 1.29 (mid-size / all private).
- **GSA CALC+ 'controller' labor-category rates (median about $59/hr) as a fractional-controller benchmark** The category is mostly document, configuration and project controllers rather than financial controllers, so it is the wrong population.
- **Large-employer ECEC multipliers (500+ workers: 1.545; management occupations: 1.48) applied to small-business staff** Measured, but for a different population. Using them would overstate small-client savings by 10-15%.
- **Vendor 'true cost of an SDR' figures (e.g., outsourced-SDR agency cost-of-hiring pages such as stealthagents.com)** Vendor marketing with no disclosed method. Use BLS 41-3091 or Bridge Group OTE plus the ECEC load instead, and leave tools and management out.
- **Search-snippet rate claims: 'average accountant charges $150-$300/hr (2026)', 'bookkeepers charge $40-$100/hr', 'NSA: messy records add $117 to your accounting bill'** Seen only in search summaries of vendor blogs. Not traced to a primary source with a method.
- **'Office workers are productive only about 3 hours a day' and similar viral productivity figures** Commonly attributed to a UK consumer-brand poll, not verified in this pass. Self-reported and not US data, so it must not drive productive-hours or AI-capacity assumptions.

### Time on repetitive work

- **AI assistants make document work 'moderately faster' (Dillon et al.)** This is v1 wording only. The current v4 finds no statistically significant change in Word time and 'we do not detect shifts in the quantity or composition of workers' tasks'.
- **AI email assistants save '3 hours (25%) of email time per week' (Dillon et al., April 2025)** Version 1 of the paper. Later versions with more firms revised it to 2.0 h (17%) for users and 1.4 h (12%) for everyone offered access. Cite arXiv v4 (Nov 2025).
- **'An AI assistant saves every worker 2 hours a week on email'** 2.0 h is the LATE for workers who used Copilot at least once in months 4-6, at very large firms. The average for everyone given access was 1.4 h. State the population and that some authors worked at Microsoft.
- **'Email takes 29% of the workweek' (11.7 h / 40 h) presented as a general fact** 11.7 h is session time (which may overstate) for knowledge workers at 66 very large firms in a Copilot pilot, and it includes about 2.2 h outside working hours. If cited, say 'averaged 11.7 hours a week in email sessions' with the population.
- **'A typical worker spends 4-6 hours a week on email' (inferred from Microsoft's 8.8 h top-quartile figure)** Microsoft does not say this. Its 8.8 h figure covers all commercial Microsoft 365 users including frontline workers, and Microsoft says knowledge workers' share is greater.
- **'Workers waste 58% of their day' (Asana)** Asana's 58% is self-reported time on coordination ('work about work') from a vendor survey, not measured waste. If used: 'knowledge workers report spending 58% of their day on work about work (Asana survey, 2023)'.
- **'Automation cuts month-end close by 1.6 (or 3.6) days'** APQC's figures are gaps between peers at one point in time (median 6.4, top quartile 4.8 or less, bottom quartile 10 or more), not measured effects of automation. Present any close improvement as an illustrative target.
- **Using these time-use benchmarks to claim the AI consulting service delivers 'similar results' to a consulting firm** None of these sources measures consulting deliverables or outcomes. Email and admin time savings are not evidence of equivalent advisory quality.
- **McKinsey Global Institute (2012): workers spend '28% of the workweek' reading and answering email, '19%' searching and gathering information, '650 hours a year' on email** Still unverified: McKinsey's page timed out again in this pass. It is also a 2012 modeled estimate for 'interaction workers', not a measurement. Use the 2025 measured figure (with its caveats) instead.
- **'SCORE 2025 Small Business Owner Time Allocation Survey': owners spend 23 hours/week on admin (n=2,100), including 5.8 h bookkeeping, 4.2 h payroll/HR, 3.7 h compliance** Found only on an SEO aggregator. The researcher found no such survey on score.org, and no primary source could be traced. Treat it as untraceable.
- **'QuickBooks Time Study 2025: entrepreneurs spend 16-20 hours/week on admin'; 'Cornerstone Advisors Q4 2023: 20.5 hours/week of accounting work';** These come from the same aggregator chain and could not be traced to a primary report with a disclosed method.
- **NSBA 2025 Small Business Taxation Survey: '77% of owners spend more than 40 hours/year on federal tax compliance; 32% more than 80'** The primary report is members-only, and the readable secondary article gave no sample or method. Use the IRS burden tables instead (small entities average 40-50 h/year).
- **APQC: median monthly close is 6.0 days across 11,223 companies** Not found in any retrieved page. The traceable APQC figure is 6.4 calendar days median, 2,300 organizations, published 2018.
- **Ventana Research (2022): 59% of businesses take six business days to close the books** According to the citing source, it rests on 48 mostly large companies, which is too small and unrepresentative. Not checked further.
- **'88% of spreadsheets contain errors' (Panko)** Attributed to a 2008 revision whose host site is offline. The verified figures are 'at least 86%' (Panko 2000) and '94% of 85 spreadsheets' (Panko 2015), both based on audits from 1987-2001.
- **'94% of SMB workers perform repetitive tasks; 38% do data entry' (Zapier 2021)** Vendor survey whose retrieved text discloses no sample, fieldwork firm or dates.
- **Gusto: 'save 48 hours per year on payroll' / 'small businesses spend nearly five hours per pay period on payroll'** Marketing claims based on surveys of Gusto's own customers, so the sample is biased.
- **'Small businesses spend 14 hours a week chasing payments'** Generalized from a November 2021 QuickBooks/Wakefield survey of 2,000 owners of businesses with 25-200 employees.
- **'Knowledge workers spend 2.5 hours a day (roughly 20-30% of their time) searching for information' (IDC)** Usually traced to an IDC white paper from about 2001. Unverified and too old. If a search-time point is needed, use Microsoft WTI 2023 ('62% ... struggle with too much time spent searching'), labeled as a self-reported survey and not converted into hours.
- **'Workers spend 209 minutes a day on work email' (Adobe)** The minutes figure appeared only in secondary snippets. Adobe's own page says 'three-plus hours a day', self-reported checking time. Not a model input.
- **Asana: '60%' (2021) or '62%' of the workday is 'work about work' / 'repetitive, mundane tasks'** Older editions or summary text not verified word for word. If Asana is cited at all, use the 2023 figure (58%) with the 'self-reported, vendor survey' label.
- **'Small-business owners work 52 hours a week' (Gallup), presented as current** The source is real but dates from 2005 (n=607) and measures total hours, not admin hours.

### AI time savings

- **'Same (or similar) results as a consulting firm for a fraction of the cost' for the AI consulting service** No rigorous study compares the outcomes of AI-delivered consulting with consulting-firm engagements.
- **'AI raises quality 40%' / 'consultants produced 40% higher-quality work' (Harvard/BCG study)** This comes from the 2023 working-paper materials (co-author Ethan Mollick's September 2023 summary: '40% higher quality results').
- **'Novices are 34% (or 43%) more productive with AI' applied to a client's whole team** These are subgroup effects: novice support agents in the NBER version (30% in the published QJE), and the lowest-scoring consultants (43% in the 2023 working-paper materials; the 2026 HBS summary reports 31% for bottom-half vs 11% for top-half performers).
- **'Developers are 55.8% faster' / 'Copilot users finish tasks in 26-73% of the time'** Single or hand-picked lab tasks built to suit AI: the 55.8% is one JavaScript task with 95 recruited freelancers (95% CI 21-89%), and Microsoft itself says the overall speed increase 'is likely to be much lower'.
- **'AI makes experienced developers 19% slower' stated as a current fact** That METR result used early-2025 tools with 16 developers. METR's February 2026 update says developers are 'likely' sped up more by early-2026 tools, with raw speedup estimates of 4-18% (wide confidence intervals, selection bias).
- **'AI speeds up tasks by about 80%' (Anthropic's estimate from Claude conversations)** A vendor estimate in which the model itself guesses how long tasks would take without AI. It excludes validation and work outside the chat, per the authors' own caveat. It is not a measured client outcome.
- **'Copilot saves 26 minutes a day / 13 working days a year' (UK government trial)** Self-reported with no control group and computed from range midpoints. The department-level DBT evaluation found no evidence that time savings led to improved productivity (though it collected limited data on that), and its small observed test found Excel anal…
- **Self-reported or perceived time savings presented as measured savings (worker surveys, client anecdotes, vendor ROI studies)** Users overestimated savings about 3x (36 vs 12 minutes measured), and METR's developers believed they were 20% faster while measured 19% slower. Use measured RCT or telemetry estimates, or apply a haircut (base 0.5).
- **Hours saved x hourly wage presented as guaranteed cash savings or ROI** Saved time has not shown up in earnings or recorded hours (precise null effects in Danish registers), about 9 in 10 executives report no productivity impact so far, Copilot users replied to the same number of emails and completed the same number of documents,…
- **'Employer encouragement raises AI benefits by 10-40%' as a causal claim** This was a cross-sectional association in the May 2025 version of Humlum & Vestergaard, and the March 2026 revision dropped the figure. Saying encouraged use is 'associated with' higher reported benefits is as far as the evidence goes.
- **'AI chatbots lift sales 16%' generalized to any business or to total store sales** The 16.3% is the sales effect among about 44,000 shoppers who sent a pre-sale question, against an auto-reply saying service was unavailable, on one very large cross-border platform. It is not a store-wide figure.
- **'AI lets accountants serve 55% more clients and close books 7.5 days faster' as an expected client outcome** An observational association among self-selected users of AI accounting tools (working paper), not a causal estimate. The 55% figure was found only in a third-party copy of the abstract.
- **'MIT study: 95% of generative-AI pilots fail or deliver zero return'** Non-representative method (interviews with 52 organizations, survey responses from 153 senior leaders at industry conferences, and a review of about 300 public initiatives, per press coverage of the report) and widely misquoted.
- **'AI agents resolve 70-80%+ of customer inquiries end-to-end', or press-release claims that 'AI does the work of N agents'** No rigorous support found. In the only randomized agentic-AI field experiment, AI-eligible chats were 5.8% of volume and only 35% of those were resolved without a human, roughly 2% of all chats, with lower ratings on fully AI-handled chats.
- **'AI-personalized cold emails get 2-3x the replies' or '6.5-12% reply rates'** These come from vendor and agency blogs with no disclosed sampling or method, and no randomized study was found. Reply-rate baselines belong to the sales-benchmark domain.
- **'4 in 5 small businesses using AI report 20%+ productivity gains' / '43% of AI-using SMBs report higher revenue'** Vendor and industry surveys of self-selected AI users with self-reported outcomes, not traced to a primary source with a disclosed method. The rigorous firm survey (NBER 'Firm Data on AI') finds about 9 in 10 executives report no productivity impact so far.
- **Whole-job replacement, '10x productivity' or 'an AI employee' framing** End-to-end automation of real paid freelance projects was 2.5% in October 2025 and 15.8% for the best model in July 2026, and most output was below professional quality. Whole-job time savings among users average about 3-5% of hours (self-reported).
- **Benchmark-derived speed or cost multiples (e.g., from OpenAI's GDPval) or 'share of work time that is automatable' figures presented as client savings** GDPval's abstract analyzes models' potential, 'when paired with human oversight', to do tasks 'cheaper and faster than unaided experts', on one-shot graded deliverables, and it is vendor research. Technical-potential estimates are not realized savings.
- **Economy-wide productivity estimates (e.g., +1.1-1.3% US labor productivity) presented as a per-client result** These are aggregate figures built from self-reported time savings across all workers (1.1% in the February 2025 St. Louis Fed article; 'up to 1.3%' in November 2025).

- **'Law firms respond to web leads in 13 minutes' (Hennessey 2025) stated without context** It is the median among responding firms, apparently excluding the 26% that never replied within seven days, in a curated, lead-hungry sample of law firms. - **'Klarna's AI resolves two-thirds of customer service' / 'AI replaced 700 agents' / 'AI matches human CSAT'** Klarna said the assistant 'handled' two-thirds of chats, did 'the equivalent work of 700 full-time agents' and was 'on par with human agents' on satisfaction. All self-reported, no method, and not a resolution rate.
- **'AI agents resolve 51-86% of conversations' (Intercom Fin: 51%, 56% within 30 days, 76% average, 'up to 86%') as an expected result for SMB clients** Vendor figures that count 'assumed' resolutions (the customer leaves). The 2026 denominator change raised reported rates (Intercom's own example: 33% to 50%), while Automation Rate is unchanged. The strict benchmark puts Intercom at 42%.
- **Presenting the Gorgias benchmark's 46% field average or 48% median vendor as what AI resolves for any business, or as a current figure** Both figures are accurate for SaaStr's Oct 6, 2026 snapshot (the researcher's earlier note that the body does not support 48% was wrong).
- **'Agentforce resolves 84-85% of queries' as an industry benchmark** Salesforce reporting on its own product on its own documentation-heavy help site, with no definition of resolution. Not generalizable.
- **'Agentic AI will autonomously resolve 80% of common customer service issues by 2029' (Gartner, Mar 5, 2025)** Retrieved and confirmed as a forecast ('By 2029, agentic AI will autonomously resolve 80% of common customer service issues without human intervention, leading to a 30% reduction in operational costs'), not a measurement.

- **After-hours call-share figures: '60% of inbound calls to local service businesses come outside 9-to-5 (BIA Advisory Local Commerce Monitor)', '67% of home service requests happen outside business hour…** None could be traced to a primary source with a method. Use Zocdoc (48% of online bookings) for online channels, and treat the phone after-hours share as a client input.

- **'Average residential CSR book rate is 65-75%; top call centers book above 85%' (attributed to ServiceTitan), or the 42% typical rate applied to small shops** The 65-75% figure appeared only in a search summary. The ServiceTitan post actually retrieved reports a 42% typical call booking rate (June 2022), and only 24% for shops with fewer than five techs.

### Paid marketing

- **“Google Ads returns $2 for every $1 spent” / “the average Google Ads ROAS is 2:1”** Google's Economic Impact methodology states an assumption, not a measurement: “for every $1 businesses spend on Google Ads, they generate an average of $2 in profit” (from Varian, 2009). It is profit, not ROAS, and comes from the seller of the ads.
- **“Businesses receive $8 in profit for every $1 spent on Google Ads”** Built from three stated assumptions on Google's methodology page (economicimpact.google/methodology): $2 profit per $1 of ads (Varian 2009), five organic clicks per ad click, and organic clicks worth about 70% of ad clicks.
- **“Small businesses waste 25% of their PPC budget” (stated as a current fact)** A 2013 WordStream vendor analysis of 500 SMB AdWords accounts with no stated definition or method for 'waste' (the article lists causes only). It predates current match types, Smart Bidding and Performance Max. Use only as historical context.
- **“39% of ad spend is wasted” (from Doctor Ads' 39.4% median)** The author calls it an upper bound on visibly unproductive spend. Zero attributed conversions is not zero value, small accounts show zeros by chance, and the sample is 84 advertisers from one consultant.
- **WordStream 2025 Meta lead-ad industry CPLs such as Restaurants & Food $3.16 or Attorneys & Legal Services $18.17** Cells contain as few as 2 campaigns, and the values swing implausibly (-93% and -77% year over year).
- **WordStream 2026 Meta industry figures presented as precise industry norms** Minimum 4 campaigns per subcategory, and the page has copy errors (end date 'June 30, 2028', Meta campaigns called 'search advertising campaigns', CVR described as both an 'almost 11% increase' and a '0.98% decrease').
- **Triple Whale “median blended ROAS 2.87x across 35,000+ brands in 2025” and “Meta-reported ROAS 1.86x vs blended 2.87x”** Seen only on third-party sites. Triple Whale's report is gated and the figure could not be verified on a Triple Whale page. Verified alternatives: Meta median 1.88, Google median 3.27 (Aug 2025 to Jul 2026), and a 2024 overall median of 2.04.
- **Meta Advantage+ shopping “17% lower CPA and 32% higher ROAS”, or “$4.52 ROAS vs $3.70 (22% lift)”** The 17% / 32% figures do appear on a Meta primary page (Meta for Developers blog, Oct 31, 2024: “Boost return on ad spend (ROAS) by 32% and reduce cost per action (CPA) by 17%”), but with no method, sample or test design.
- **“99% of AI Max impressions have 0 conversions” (Monks, via PPC Land)** Anecdotal agency post with no sample definition or method.
- **Single case studies such as L'Oréal's “2X higher conversion rate at a 31% lower cost-per-conversion” with AI Max, or agency claims of 40-70% CPL cuts** Hand-picked best cases with no control group. They don't generalize to SMB accounts and would breach the 'realistic numbers' rule.
- **Opascope: unmanaged negative keywords drive “15 to 30%” of non-brand budget waste** Vendor marketing claim with no disclosed sample or method.
- **Rules of thumb such as “ad fatigue sets in above a frequency of 3” or the “rule of 7”** No primary source with a method was found. Peer-reviewed evidence (Chae, Bruno & Feinberg 2019) shows wear-out varies by person (about 24% of users weary in one campaign), not a universal threshold.
- **Schmidt & Eisend (2015): “brand attitude peaks at about ten exposures”** Could not verify at the source (publisher page blocked; no abstract in Crossref). It is also a lab-setting meta-analysis, not a digital frequency-cap benchmark.
- **Bing's “revenue per search up 10% to 25% each year” from experimentation (HBR 2017) as something an SMB can expect** Came from hundreds of experiments a month at Bing's scale. SMB accounts lack the traffic to detect small effects (a 10% conversion-rate lift needs about 36,000 clicks).
- **Industry CPL computed as CPC divided by conversion rate from WordStream tables** Medians are computed per metric and don't combine. For example, Shopping 2026 gives $4.14 / 4.01% = $103 against a published CPL of $49.40, and Legal gives $178 against $131.63.
- **Quoting WordStream industry CPLs to the cent, or saying legal/home-services CPL was “unchanged year over year”** WordStream's tables contain many identical-to-the-cent values: Legal $131.63 and Home $90.92 in both 2025 and 2026; 2026 Health & Fitness = Career & Employment ($67.36) and Automotive-For Sale = Sports & Recreation ($44.26);
- **Citing LocaliQ's '2026 Search Advertising Benchmarks' blog page as the source** It mirrors WordStream 2026 data but omits the methodology note (sample size, window, medians). Cite wordstream.com/blog/2026-google-ads-benchmarks instead.
- **Applying ANA's “36 cents of every dollar reaches the consumer” to small-business Google or Meta accounts** It measures open-web programmatic display for 21 major advertisers and includes transaction fees. It does not describe search or SMB social accounts.
- **Public claim of “10-15% lower CPL” (or 15% as the typical/base outcome), as recommended in the earlier draft** No study measures the average gain from 60-90 days of optimization of SMB accounts.
- **WordStream: “adding just one negative keyword to your search or campaigns can triple your conversion rates”** Vendor statement on the 2026 benchmarks page with no method or sample given there; implausible as a general expectation.
- **LocaliQ: “Our clients are seeing greater improvements than the broader industry” (CTR stability credited to the LocaliQ platform)** Vendor self-assessment with no comparison method; it also signals that WordStream's benchmark sample is LocaliQ client campaigns.
- **Optimizely: “+20.6% average test impact” for Optimizely AI customers, or a “median uplift of 57%” for multivariate tests** Vendor product claims with self-selected users. The 57% is the median of 8 winners among 44 multivariate tests, a textbook winner's-curse figure.
- **“Meta leads are 2.4x cheaper than Google leads” ($27.39 vs $66.69)** Different samples (452 Meta lead campaigns vs 13,474 search campaigns) and conversion definitions (instant forms vs site leads, calls). Lead quality is not compared.
- **Using Gordon et al.'s 5% median lower-funnel lift as “ads cause only 5% of sales” or as a fixed haircut to platform ROAS** The lift is relative to control-group conversions in lift studies that advertisers chose to run on Facebook, and lower-funnel outcomes include registrations and app activations as well as purchases.
- **Calling Triple Whale's ROAS/CPA medians “platform-reported” or “blended”** Triple Whale's benchmark pages don't state the attribution basis; describe them as attributed ROAS in Triple Whale's customer panel.
- **Presenting Chae, Bruno & Feinberg's “as much as 15%” as a conversion or sales gain from frequency capping** It measured ad deployment for website visits in a single display campaign; 15% is the maximum for those data, not a typical conversion effect.

### Sales outreach

- **Advanced personalization lifts cold email reply rates to ~17-18%, against ~7-9% for basic templates (Woodpecker blog; SmartReach says 'near 18%').** No dataset, time period or method is disclosed, and the figures are far above every measured platform average (3-4.5% total replies). Use Hunter's measured +56% (5.6% vs 3.6%) instead, labeled as correlational.
- **AI-personalized first lines lift reply rates (any blanket claim that the agent's AI-written personalization raises replies).** The only large 2026 dataset that isolates AI-written openers (sales.co, 1.28M contacts) found no lift: 0.53% vs 1.00% human replies, 0.15% vs 0.56% positive, and a coin flip within the same client (10 of 19).
- **A typical 15-20% of replies convert to meetings, so a 5.5% reply rate yields 8-11 meetings per 1,000 sends (Instantly, AI Sales Agent Benchmarks 2026 blog).** No data or method is given for the reply-to-meeting rate. 8-11 meetings per 1,000 sends is roughly 50-70x Belkins' measured 0.16 appointments per 1,000 emails, and in classified datasets about half of raw replies are automated.
- **sales.co's 2.09% reply rate x 14.1% positive share ≈ 0.29% of contacts reply positively.** Mixed denominators: 14.1% is a share of 61,770 reply messages, 2.09% a share of contacts. Use sales.co's de-duplicated per-contact series (0.25% positive, falling to 0.18% in September 2026).
- **Platform reply rates (Hunter 4.5%, Instantly 3.43%) as the share of prospects who are interested or become leads.** They count all replies (neither vendor says auto-replies are excluded). In classified datasets 45-54% of replies are automated and most human replies are refusals. Measured interested replies are about 2-4 per 1,000 prospects in 2025-2026.
- **Top-decile reply rates (Instantly 10.7%+, Hunter's 6.2% best segment, OneAway's best client) as what a client should expect.** These are best-case subgroups and observational. Present them only as ceilings, never as expected results.
- **1-3% of cold emails sent turn into booked meetings (SmartReach).** At SmartReach's own ~3.4% reply rate, 30-90% of all replies would have to become meetings. It contradicts measured end-to-end data (Belkins: over 1,200 appointments from 7.5M emails, ≈0.016%) and measured interested-reply rates of 0.17-0.24% per email.
- **Saleshandy's 30.31M-email statistics: 4.5% average reply rate, 3-5% average positive reply rate, 15-20% reply rates for campaigns under 200 prospects, 2-3 meetings per 100 emails for top campaigns.** Internally inconsistent: two incompatible data windows (January-June vs July-October 2026), and a positive rate its own total reply rate cannot support. The top-performer claims have no method and are over 100x the measured Belkins rate.

- **Outlook requires SPF/DKIM/DMARC from all business senders over 5,000 emails a day and rejects non-compliant mail to Microsoft 365 customers.** Microsoft's post says the rule applies to 'Outlook.com - our consumer service' (hotmail.com, live.com, outlook.com addresses), not Microsoft 365 business tenants.
- **Gmail's sender rules govern email to business prospects on Google Workspace.** Google's FAQ: 'The Email sender guidelines don’t apply to messages sent to Google Workspace accounts.' They apply to personal Gmail accounts, although following them is still best practice.
- **Ebsta's CRM data show a typical B2B win rate of 20-25%.** Ebsta's text says 'you may expect' 20-25%. It is a rhetorical expectation, not a measured average, and the report publishes no overall win rate.
- **The average B2B sales win rate is 21% and the close rate 29% (attributed to HubSpot).** Not found on HubSpot's State of Sales report page, which gives no absolute win rate (only that 91% say win rates are stable or improving). The figures appear only on aggregator sites.
- **15% of cold emails convert to meetings with interest-based CTAs (Gong Labs study of 304,174 emails).** Not verifiable on a Gong primary page; only third-party blogs carry it. A 15% email-to-meeting rate is implausible against measured rates.
- **Cold email costs $152.73 per meeting against $2,777.78 for calling (Instantly blog).** No inputs or method are disclosed; it is a vendor marketing comparison.
- **Fully managed IT averages $145 per user per month, per a survey of 412 MSPs and buyers.** The survey could not be traced. Use Kaseya's 984-respondent price bands instead.
- **Commodity janitorial contracts run $0.50-$2.00 per sq ft per month (market-research summary pages).** No method is given, and the range is roughly 5-10x ISSA's $0.09-$0.17. It likely confuses annual and monthly figures.

- **Sales reps spend only 28% of their week selling (Salesforce).** Superseded: it comes from the 5th edition (2022 survey). The 7th edition (Aug-Sep 2025, n=4,050) reports 40%. Do not compare editions as a trend, because the samples differ.

The only primary figure found is the IRS SOI blended 33.9% for plumbing and HVAC contractors.

Interchange (roughly 1.5-2.6% for common Visa credit categories) is set by the networks and is not negotiable for small merchants, so achievable savings are usually a small share of total fees.

- **'AI-driven forecasting cuts inventory 20-50%' and similar inventory-reduction claims** Consultant and vendor marketing claims with no public sample or method found. The model uses a small, explicitly judgmental 0-8% range and treats inventory as upside.

### Consulting costs (for the AI Consulting comparison)

- **Any 'same result' or 'similar result' claim, e.g. 'Our AI consulting delivers similar results to a consulting firm at a fraction of the cost'** No study shows equivalent outcomes. Peer-reviewed work finds single AI strategy evaluations inconsistent and biased, and a 2025 Big Four report with AI-generated errors was partly refunded.
- **'Big Four commercial rates run $230-$1,575/hr' or '$815/hr is a Big Four blended rate'** Those figures come from bankruptcy filings by restructuring specialists (Alvarez & Marsal, FTI, AlixPartners), not the Big Four, and $815.10 is A&M's blend in a large liquidation. No public record of Big Four commercial non-distressed rates was found.
- **'GSA small-business median for management consultants is $161.65/hr (1,092 rates)'** The keyword query also matched vendor names, adding 163 non-consultant jobs. Use the cleaned figure: $164.26/hr median, n = 929 (25th pct $128.97; 90th $252.35).
